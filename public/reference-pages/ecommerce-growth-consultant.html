Your growth problem is not a traffic problem

You are already paying for traffic. Ads are running, people are clicking — and somewhere between the click and the order, most of them disappear. Each point in that line leaks a little: the landing page doesn’t match the ad, the product page hides the shipping cost, the cart introduces doubt at exactly the wrong moment.

The real problem is that each point is owned by someone different — or by nobody. Your media buyer stops at the ad. Your developer stops at the theme. Nobody owns the line. Growth stalls not because you need more traffic, but because the traffic you have keeps falling through gaps no one is watching.

Who this is for

  • — A US or UK DTC brand in apparel, accessories, or home.
  • — You are running paid traffic, Meta ads especially, and getting orders.
  • — You want one person who can see the ads, the landing pages, and the store together.

Not a fit: pre-launch stores with no traffic data yet, anyone wanting a full rebrand, and anyone who needs guaranteed growth numbers — I will not promise those. And if your only problem is the store itself, my Shopify CRO consulting page is the sharper fit.

What does an ecommerce growth consultant actually do?

An ecommerce growth consultant owns the full journey from first click to repeat purchase — auditing and improving Meta ads, landing pages, Shopify product pages, cart, checkout, and post-purchase touchpoints so traffic converts and customers come back.

That is different from hiring an agency: one senior operator does the audit and ships the fixes — no account manager relaying messages. Different from a media buyer: the work doesn’t stop at the ad. And different from a CRO consultant: the work doesn’t stop at the store.

What it is not: not ad account management, not a redesign service, and not guaranteed lift percentages.

My method: one growth line, end to end

I treat your whole funnel as one thing: Meta ad → landing page → product page → cart → checkout → post-purchase. Reviewed as one line, because a sharper ad doesn’t help a confusing landing page, and a clean store doesn’t save a weak offer.

That’s only possible because my background covers the line: Meta ads, landing pages, Shopify design and development, ecommerce UX. Most people own one slice — ads or CRO or dev — and the slices don’t talk to each other. You can see the range of that work in my portfolio.

Growth is a connected system

Your funnel is one line: the ad makes a promise, the click carries intent, the landing page receives it, the product page sells it, the offer and trust signals close it, and checkout collects it. Break any link and the whole line leaks.

That is why growth work that only fixes one stage keeps disappointing. A sharper ad pointed at a confusing landing page just buys more expensive bounces. A beautiful store with a weak offer converts a trickle of expensive traffic. A strong offer with no trust near the buy button stalls at the final step. Each stage multiplies the next — so the work has to diagnose the line in order, not the stage someone happens to sell.

Most people own one slice: ads or landing pages or the store. The slices don’t talk to each other — and the gaps between them are where your growth goes. One operator across the whole line is the thesis of this page: the handoffs get owned instead of dropped.

Bottleneck diagnosis: find the one constraint

A line converts at the speed of its tightest constraint. The bottleneck is the single stage costing you the most sales right now — the message mismatch between ad and landing page, the product page that hides shipping, the checkout step where buyers stall. Every engagement starts by finding it, because fixing the bottleneck moves revenue and fixing everything at once hides what worked.

  • — Walk the line as a buyer. Ad to checkout, mobile and desktop. I document where buyers hesitate, stall, or drop — with screenshots from your actual pages.
  • — Name the constraint. Out of everything found, one stage is the binding constraint: the leak that, fixed, unlocks the most revenue. That is what gets fixed first.
  • — Fix in order, verify after. The bottleneck first, then the next. Every change checked after it ships — so you always know what moved the number.

The ranked fix list you approve on day three of the sprint is this diagnosis written down: every finding ordered by expected revenue impact, bottleneck first, so the work starts where the money is.

An ecommerce growth consultant owns the whole line — ads, landing pages, and store — for a founder who doesn’t want to coordinate three vendors and watch the gaps between them.

If you know the problem is only in the store, the Shopify Conversion Fix Sprint is the sharper starting point. If the question is bigger — who should run our ecommerce growth — you’re reading the right page.

Shopify collaborator access — I’ll specify the exact permissions before you approve anything. Read-only access to your ad account so I can review the journey from the ad side, and GA4 read access if you have it. Mostly I need your time during the sprint: quick answers to quick questions.

How much traffic do I need before hiring a growth consultant?

Enough to read decisions from. If you cannot tell whether a change moved sales because weeks pass with barely any buyers, the constraint is traffic, not the store — growth work can still find leaks, but you will not be able to measure the fix. Stores with steady ad or organic traffic get the most from this work.

Will you redesign my store?

No redesign. Fixes and builds work inside your existing theme and design language. If a visual change would genuinely move the number, I’ll explain why and get your approval first.
